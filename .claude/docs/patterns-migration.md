# The TV Maze → TMDB migration: one-shot passes and their residue

> Extracted from `.claude/CLAUDE.md` (2026-08-19) to keep the always-loaded file under the context limit.
> Read before touching any `tvbf/jobs/*` migration pass, `catalog/seasons.py`, `catalog/episodes.py`, or anything reasoning about copied (`tmdb_id IS NULL`) rows. Every pass here has already run in production.

These are load-bearing and easy to get wrong.

**A copied TV Maze special gets a negative episode number.** `catalog.episode.episode_number` is NOT NULL (a TMDB special is season 0 with a real number, audit D2), but `tvmaze.episode.number` is NULL for a special — 27,498 rows, 156 of them watched. `tvmaze/catalog_copy.py` (deleted by NEU-1051) preserved the real season number and numbers those episodes **-1, -2, … within the season**, with the floor taken from the most negative number already in `catalog` so a re-run appends instead of restarting. Two things make negatives the right answer and both are easy to undo by accident: numbering them contiguously after the season's highest real number **collides**, because the daily keeps adding real episodes until cutover and nothing catches it (no unique key on `(show_id, season_number, episode_number)`, and `ON CONFLICT (id) DO NOTHING` leaves the earlier row alone); and a contiguous number reads as a real episode, where a negative says the value was invented. These rows keep `tmdb_id IS NULL` until **NEU-1146 retires them** — they were NEU-1045's residue, and "permanently" was true only while a locally-authored row was a sanctioned resting state (ADR-0008). ADR-0012 withdrew that: a synthetic special either title-matches a TMDB special in season 0, which is the *same episode at a different address*, or it is deleted with its watch rows. 21,012 of them were classified as this cause on 2026-08-14, 155 user-touched. Separately, the copy carries across 2,298 duplicate `(show, season, number)` triples that already exist upstream — 13 shows number two seasons the same — and neither introduces nor repairs them.

**The season list is the one payload the repoint changes, and `catalog/seasons.py` is the decision.** NEU-1119 kept 18,339 copied seasons TMDB has no counterpart for, across 4,396 shows, so a show's seasons are not what TMDB alone would give. The read rule is **one row per `(show_id, season_number)`, preferring the row that carries a `tmdb_id`**, ties broken on the lowest id — applied by `browse_queries.get_show_seasons`, by `season_repo.unaired_for_shows`, which dedupes **before** filtering to unaired rather than after (a doubled number whose ingested row has already aired would otherwise surface as upcoming on the strength of the copy), and by `feed_service._season_names_by_key`, which names a `watched_season` roll-up (NEU-1132) and is the one caller that loads seasons by `(show_id, season_number)` pair rather than a whole show's worth. Two things it deliberately does not do. It does **not** prefer "the ingested set when a show has one": TMDB models the *Will & Grace* revival as a separate series, so its seasons 9–11 exist only as copied rows and two carry real watch history — preferring the ingested set would hide episodes a user has marked watched. And it does **not** collapse the year-vs-ordinal split (10,193 seasons across 929 shows where TV Maze numbers by calendar year and TMDB by ordinal): the two schemes never collide, so no rule keyed on the number can tell them apart, and the only rule that could would hide episodes that exist nowhere else. None of the 929 is tracked by a user, so the residue is untidy for nobody and lossless for everybody. **NEU-1146 ends the residue this rule exists for**: once `task retire:orphans` has run, every copied season is either superseded by an ingested row or deleted, so no `(show_id, season_number)` carries two. The rule itself stays — it is what keeps the read paths correct *while* orphans exist, a delta can create fresh ones, and preferring the `tmdb_id`-bearing row is the right answer either way. Do not delete it on the strength of a clean count.

**A copied special's negative number sorts last within its season, and that takes an explicit term.** `catalog/episodes.py` owns `EPISODE_ORDER`, used by `get_show_episodes` and by the three orderings in `episode_repo`. A plain `ORDER BY season_number, episode_number` moves all 27,498 copied specials from the end of their season to the **front**, because Postgres sorted the TV Maze NULLs they replaced last on ASC and sorts -1 first. Season 0 needs the same treatment one grain up, and for the opposite reason: a TMDB-native special sorts *ahead* of season 1 by upstream's own model, so `EPISODE_ORDER` leads with `(season_number == 0).asc()` and `catalog/seasons.py`'s `SEASON_ORDER` is the season-grain twin (NEU-1062). `deduped` re-sorts in Python, so the query ordering alone would not decide what a caller sees — the two have to agree.

**A special is two things, `catalog/episodes.py` is the only module that decides, and each call site names its own predicate.** `IS_SPECIAL` is season 0 **or** a negative `episode_number`; `IS_COPIED_SPECIAL` is only the second (NEU-1062). Neither kind counts toward show-level progress, or a user who watched every regular episode never reads 100% — but **per-season** math strips only the copied ones, so season 0's own row can report its own contents. Three sites deliberately filter nothing and each fails silently in the dangerous direction if "tidied": `episode_repo.get_by_id` serves a special's own episode page, `episode_repo.list_episode_ids_for_show` backs *un*-marking a whole show (excluding anything orphans those watch rows), and `episode_watch_repo.list_episode_ids_for_show` backs the show page's ticks, where a watched special must still read as watched. A shared `regular_episodes()` base selectable was rejected for exactly that asymmetry — it would need overriding at 5 of 12 sites, and the site that forgot would have no test. `tests/integration/app/repos/test_specials_ledger.py` holds the whole table and **fails on any query added to either repo without a row in it**; that tripwire is the mechanism, not the coverage. Bulk-marking a show skips specials (so "mark all watched" reaches 100%), and marking season 0 explicitly still works. **Marking and un-marking are deliberately asymmetric at both grains, which is two pairs of functions rather than one shared query**: marking reads the filtered `list_aired_episode_ids_for_show` / `list_episode_ids_for_season`, un-marking reads the unfiltered `list_episode_ids_for_show` / `list_all_episode_ids_for_season`. Sharing one filtered query between a grain's mark and unmark leaves a special ticked by hand unreachable from the thing it hangs inside — the spec's ledger listed one function per grain, and its §4 prose ("un-marking is unchanged at every grain") is the half that is right. The friend feed is deliberately left alone: past this ticket every special watch is a deliberate act, and suppressing it would misreport what somebody did — which is why **the activity-event collapse must never be wider than the mark it substitutes for**. `activity_event_repo.delete_episode_events_for_season` / `delete_episode_and_season_events_for_show` carry the same predicate as the marking query at their grain; unfiltered, they delete the feed item for a special somebody ticked by hand while its watch row stands, and the `watched_season` / `watched_show` event standing in for it never covered that episode. The ledger test spans all five modules that read `catalog.episode` on a user's behalf — `episode_repo`, `episode_watch_repo`, `season_repo`, `activity_event_repo` and, since NEU-1103 gave it its first query that is not handed ids, `episode_rating_repo` — because that pair is exactly where a narrower tripwire would have missed it. That last one is the ledger's only `EXCLUDE_NOTHING` at show grain: a rating is a sentence somebody typed about an episode rather than progress, so the taste signal's episode-rating mean counts a rated special where every other show-level aggregate strips it.

**Three response fields survive the repoint as permanent nulls, and one keeps a name that no longer describes it.** `web_channel` (TMDB draws no broadcaster/streamer distinction — `catalog.network` absorbs both), `SeasonOut.end_date` and `EpisodeOut.airtime` (no TMDB counterpart at all), and `PersonOut`'s `country_*` / `birthday` / `deathday` (behind a per-person request the credits ingest does not make) are all null forever; each key stays in the payload because removing it is a contract change the SPA has no ticket for. `ShowDetail.tvmaze_updated` keeps its name and now carries the epoch of `coalesce(tmdb_synced_at, ingested_at)` — a legacy alias, because it is a live `?sort=` key and an SPA type, so renaming it is a coordinated change across two repos for a field whose meaning survives. `catalog/schemas.py` is the one place any of this is decided; a bare `model_validate(row)` would silently fill an unmapped field with its default instead of failing, which is why every builder names its fields.

**`catalog.show.is_ended` is a generated column, so it expires after an UPDATE.** Reading it back from an ORM object the same async session just updated is a lazy load, which raises `MissingGreenlet`. Production never hits this — every show reaching a response comes from a `select()` — but a test that mutates a seeded `Show` and then calls a read path needs `await session.refresh(show)` first.

**The `tmdb_id` mapping resolves every ambiguity to unmatched, and that asymmetry is the design.** `tmdb/enrichment.py` runs three tiers per show, stopping at the first hit: `/find` by `tvdb_id`, `/find` by `imdb_id`, then `/search/tv` by title + year — and tier 3 accepts only when the search returned **exactly one** result overall (`total_results == 1`), that result's title folds equal to ours, and its year is within ±1. No scoring, no threshold. Shows with **no premiere date never reach tier 3 at all** (no request is spent on them). Four things follow that are easy to "improve" wrongly: a false positive silently attaches a user's watch history to the wrong show and nothing downstream catches it, while a false negative is one more row in a queue already being worked by hand — the costs are not comparable. `search_tv` deliberately sends *only* `query`; adding TMDB's `first_air_date_year` would both narrow past the ±1 and change what "exactly one result" counts. `/find` returning two series is treated as a conflict, not a coin flip. And the UPDATE carries `tmdb_id IS NULL` plus a `NOT EXISTS` guard on `uq_show_tmdb_id`, which is what makes re-running unable to rewrite an existing match and turns a duplicate TV Maze show mapping onto one TMDB series into a counted collision rather than an exception that kills the batch. `match_method` exists so tier 3 stays retractable as a batch; a bare `tmdb_id` makes exact and guessed matches indistinguishable forever.

**`match_method = 'human'` is a verdict, and `tmdb_id` says which verdict it was.** The human queue (NEU-1044) has three resolutions and two of them write the same method: an id plus `'human'` means a person confirmed that series; `tmdb_id IS NULL` plus `'human'` means a person looked and TMDB has no counterpart, so the row stays locally-authored (ADR-0008) on purpose. Four things follow. **Rejecting writes rather than leaving the row alone** — an untouched row is indistinguishable from an unreviewed one, so the queue would never empty and the cutover gate would have nothing to read. **`'human'` is deliberately outside `enrichment.MATCH_METHODS`**, which is the tiers the automated pass can produce, not the whole `ck_show_match_method` vocabulary — adding it there would put a permanent 0 in every enrichment tally. **`confirm` re-stamping a row that already holds the same id is the point, not a no-op**: that is what moves a guess-checked-by-hand out of a ticket comment and into the database. And the queue is scoped to **user-touched** shows — 26k unmatched rows are the migration's expected residue, not work for a person; only the ones somebody would actually lose data over are surfaced, worst first by episode-watch count.

**The season grain is deduplicated by deleting the copy, and the delete re-asserts its own guard.** Nothing ever mapped `catalog.season`, so post-ingest every matched show carries two rows per season and `uq_season_tmdb_id` has closed the mapping window NEU-1045 used one grain down. `tmdb/season_dedupe.py` (NEU-1119) deletes the copy instead — affordable only because **nothing in `app` references a season**, which is the same asymmetry ADR-0005 draws between seasons (deletable) and shows (tombstoned). Five things are load-bearing. **The episodes are re-pointed before the delete, and the ticket predicted otherwise**: `upsert_episodes` re-points only the episodes it *writes*, and a copied episode carrying no `tmdb_id` is not one, so 2,125,419 episodes (7,120 watched) were still hanging off the doomed seasons in prod and a bare `DELETE` would have tripped `ON DELETE SET NULL` across all of them. **The `DELETE` repeats `sh.tmdb_id IS NOT NULL` rather than trusting the work list** — it is the statement that can destroy data no feed can restore, so "a season under a locally-authored show is untouched" is structural instead of a property of whichever query selected the batch. **A copied season with no ingested counterpart is kept**, not deleted, for the reason `prune_missing_seasons` carries its own `tmdb_id IS NOT NULL` guard: TMDB lacking a season number is not permission to drop TV Maze's. **Ambiguity is refused** — two `tmdb_id`-bearing rows for one number leave the copy in place, because `catalog.season` deliberately has no `UNIQUE (show_id, season_number)` and `UPDATE ... FROM` would otherwise pick one non-deterministically. And **the re-point follows the season, not the episode's own `season_number`**: the two already disagree on one prod row, and substituting the season's replacement preserves that disagreement where re-homing by the episode's number would be the pass adjudicating which field is right. There is no watermark (a row leaves the work list by being deleted), so re-run it after any later ingest or delta — a delta adding a season on a number a copied row still holds is a fresh duplicate. The inverse ordering constraint, while it still applied: **`task copy:catalog` put the rows back but not the parentage** — `_COPY_EPISODES` skipped rows already present, so a full revert was the re-copy *plus* an `UPDATE catalog.episode SET season_id` from `tvmaze.episode`, which held every original pointer. NEU-1051 deleted both the copy and that schema, so re-running the pass is now safe in one direction only. And **`still_doubled` is the honest scoreboard for the first acceptance criterion**: the pass cannot reach a doubled pair whose rows have no ingested counterpart to defer to, nor two rows the ingest itself wrote for one number, so the report names all three shapes rather than folding two of them into other counts.

**The episode grain is mapped by `(season_number, episode_number)` and nothing else, and it has to run before the ingest.** `tmdb/episode_map.py` (NEU-1045) stamps the copied `catalog.episode` rows of every matched show, because the episode upsert conflict-targets `tmdb_id` and a copied row carries none — run the ingest first and it inserts a *second* row for every episode, one holding TMDB data and one holding the preserved id `app.user_episode_watch` points at. Stamping first is what collapses those into the row users are already attached to. Five things about it are load-bearing. There is **no title or air-date fallback**: a wrong id at this grain silently re-labels a watch record, where an unmatched row is one line on a report already read by hand. **Ambiguity is never resolved by primary key** — two local rows sharing a key (TV Maze's 2,298 duplicate `(show, season, number)` triples) are both left alone, and so is an upstream id arriving under two keys, which would also break the write since one statement cannot set two rows to one `tmdb_id`. **A negative `episode_number` is excluded from the work list**, not merely unmatched: those are the copy's synthetic specials, unmappable by construction, and retrying them would re-fetch 27,498 rows' worth of shows on every run. There is **no watermark column** for the same reason `enrichment.py` needs none — a row leaves the work list by being mapped, so a re-run costs only the shows that genuinely could not map. And the report's **0%-mapped flag is a claim about the *show*** (its `tmdb_id` is probably wrong), which is why it is separate from the per-episode misses — it reads `0 of N mapped`, so a show whose fetch failed during the pass looks identical and the run's log is what tells them apart, and before any pass every matched show reads that way. The report also carries `unmirrored_watches`: a watched episode with no `catalog.episode` row at all is invisible to every other query in it, and the daily keeps creating those until cutover, so the CLI logs them as an error above the counts — same failure mode as `human_queue.unmirrored_user_touched_shows`. Both were fixed by re-running `task copy:catalog`, which NEU-1051 deleted with the schema it read — past that ticket such a row would have to be authored by hand.

**An appended `season/N` block carries no `id` — only the standalone season fetch does.** Measured 2026-08-09: the appended form's keys are `_id`, `air_date`, `episodes`, `name`, `networks`, `overview`, `poster_path`, `season_number`, `vote_average`, and `_id` is TMDB-internal, not the season id. `GET /tv/{id}/season/{n}` *does* return `id`. So **a season's identity comes from `seasons[]` on the series payload**, matched to its detail block by `season_number` — which is why `TMDBSeasonDetail.tmdb_id` is optional and `upsert_seasons` reads `series.seasons` rather than the block that carries the episodes. Taking identity from the block would give every appended season a null `tmdb_id`, and the next pass would insert all of them again.

**Episode credits ride the appended `season/N` block, so there is no episode-credits pass.** TV Maze needed a dedicated ~29h backfill over 188k seasons for guest cast and episode crew (ADR-0003; its `task credits:backfill` went with the rest of that machinery in NEU-1050), and the obvious assumption is that TMDB needs the same. It does not, and that was measured rather than inferred: `scripts/probe_tmdb_episode_credits_append.py` (2026-08-11) shows the *appended* block carrying the identical 1,460 guest stars and 7,456 crew entries as the standalone `GET /tv/{id}/season/{n}` — same key sets, no episode truncated, exact per-episode parity. Since the ingest already appends `season/N`, NEU-1040 cost a column and zero requests. Three consequences: **there is no watermark to add**, because there is no pass to resume beyond the show-grain `tmdb_synced_at`; **the delete is scoped to the episodes the payload carried**, not to the show, or a single-season re-fetch would empty every season it did not include; and **`None` vs `[]` is drawn per episode and per list**, so a payload that never mentioned an episode's credits leaves them alone where `[]` clears them. One further guard hangs off that: an episode whose crew entries were *all* skipped as unparseable is held out of the refresh rather than emptied, or "one malformed entry costs that row, not the payload" would be false at this grain. Episode credits are also **flat** where `aggregate_credits` nests: an appearance is a row, with no `roles[]`/`jobs[]` and no `episode_count`, which is why `_CreditPersonFields` holds only the person half every grain shares and `total_episode_count` sits on `TMDBAggregateEntry` below it. **Identity is not in that shared half** (NEU-1128): `TMDBCreditPerson` requires `id` and `name` for show grain, `TMDBEpisodeCreditPerson` makes both optional for episode grain, and the two are siblings rather than one widening the other. TMDB sends episode credits carrying neither — 12 of the first ~4,000 shows in NEU-1127's backfill — and under the old shared-strict shape one such entry failed `TMDBEpisode`, then `TMDBSeasonDetail`, then `TMDBSeries`, losing the whole show on every path that parses a series payload, the daily delta included. `upsert._has_person` drops the entry and logs it, and `_refresh_scope` is what stops an episode whose entries were *all* dropped being read as an upstream zero and cleared.

**`catalog.show` carries three sync watermarks, because one bit could not say what happened.** `tmdb_synced_at` means a complete payload was mirrored; `credits_synced_at` (NEU-1127) means credits were written from one. They disagree on 228,841 production rows — the full ingest ran before the credit writers merged, so every one of those shows is fully mirrored and holds no cast, crew or guest cast. `mark_series_synced` stamps **both**, because every caller reaches it through `mirror_series`, which fetches `DEFAULT_APPEND` and the season blocks and therefore wrote credits by construction; `mark_credits_synced` stamps only the second, because the backfill writes no spine and must not retire a show from the *ingest's* work list. The backfill's work list is that column and deliberately not "the show has no `show_cast` row": the predicate cannot tell *upstream has no credits* from *nobody has asked*, so it would re-fetch every credit-less series on every run and never converge — the same conflation `tmdb_synced_at` exists to avoid one grain up. `write_series_credits` is the seam that makes "writes nothing but credits" a property of the writer rather than a promise made by a caller reaching past the underscores, and it reads the episode surrogate ids back with a query rather than learning them from an `upsert_episodes` it must not perform. **`recommendations_synced_at` (NEU-1052) is the third, and the pattern is now a rule rather than a coincidence**: a namespace added after the catalog was mirrored needs its own watermark, because the backlog it creates is invisible to the ones already there and "the show has no rows in the new table" cannot represent *upstream had none* — the case that is ~8% of the long tail here. `mark_series_synced` stamps all three; `mark_recommendations_synced` stamps only its own; `write_series_recommendations` is the same seam at the same distance from its caller.

**The orphan retirement matches in four tiers and every one of them refuses ambiguity on both sides.** `tmdb/orphan_retire.py` (NEU-1146) is the tail NEU-1126's exact key could not reach, and six things about it are load-bearing. **The air date is not consulted at tiers 0 and 1** — measured, not assumed: 34 of 67 user-touched orphans with a unique same-show title match carry a non-zero date delta, up to 5,332 days, and every one was inspected and is correct, because the two catalogues date compilations and webisodes differently. Requiring it costs 11,780 population matches and 30 user-touched ones and buys nothing. **Tier 2 must not consult the title** — Will & Grace `s9e1` is `"Eleven Years Later"` against TMDB's `"11 Years Later"`, which the fold reconciles for punctuation but not for a spelled-out numeral, so the offset key pairs 17 of 17 user-touched rows where title+airdate pairs 16. **A show link needs a consistent offset, never a modal one**: an anthology counterpart legitimately has none, and tier 2b (folded title alone, inside the linked pair) is what handles it rather than a best guess. **An orphan *show* may be linked on aggregate episode-title evidence** — a unique winner over ≥2 of its titles and ≥half of those that matched anything — which reaches Cunk on Earth, whose folded show name equals nothing; that is a show-grain aggregate and not the cross-show episode title matching §3.1 forbids, since a title in twelve series hands each one vote and decides nothing. **A collision deletes the redundant row where NEU-1126 kept it** (ADR-0012 §4.2): the twin already records that viewing, so one viewing keeps one row, and the report counts it as a de-duplication rather than a loss. And **the report runs the identical matcher as the pass**, per-show in Python over titles Postgres folded — that is why the reviewed loss list is what actually happens, rather than a second query maintained alongside it, and it is the property to preserve if the planner is ever rewritten in SQL for speed.
