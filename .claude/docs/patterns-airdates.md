# Airdate correction (NEU-1145)

> Extracted from `.claude/CLAUDE.md` (2026-08-19) to keep the always-loaded file under the context limit.
> Read before touching `catalog/offsets.py`, `src/tvbf/airdates/`, `catalog.air_date_offset`, or any `air_date` / `tmdb_*_date` column.

These are load-bearing and easy to get wrong.

**An airdate is not TMDB's own value, and the raw one is stored beside it.** Since NEU-1145, `catalog.episode.air_date`, `catalog.season.air_date` and `catalog.show.first_air_date` / `last_air_date` mean *the date a US Eastern viewer saw* — TMDB's value corrected by `catalog.air_date_offset`. The cause is TMDB's own data and was measured rather than inferred: a TMDB episode carries exactly one date key, identical under every `language` and `region`, so there is no regional model to consume; what it carries is a calendar date a contributor typed, and 9pm Pacific Thursday is midnight Eastern Friday. The shift splits *within* a show, uniformly per season (Ted Lasso agrees on seasons 1-2 and not on 3-4; Silo is shifted in all three seasons and Slow Horses in none of five), so nothing on the show row predicts it and **any per-network, per-era or weekday-derived rule is rejected** — "Tue/Thu ⇒ Pacific" is right on 197 of 200 Apple TV+ rows and corrupts 17 correct Prime Video rows to fix 93. Seven things hold it up.

**The offset is a table of its own, keyed on `(show_id, season_number)`.** Not a column on `catalog.season` — which makes "the ingest never writes an offset" structural rather than remembered — and not keyed on `season.id`, because `episode.season_id` is nullable and `catalog.season` deliberately carries no `UNIQUE (show_id, season_number)`. `season_number IS NULL` is a show-wide default the job never writes; it is the operator's escape hatch, which is what keeps the two possible writers distinguishable by looking at the table.

**Every writer sets the corrected column and its `tmdb_*` twin together, from the raw value in hand.** `corrected = raw + offset` is therefore an invariant rather than a history of edits, and NULL in a twin means *this row is untouched TMDB* — true of essentially all ~6.0M dated episode rows, which keeps the coverage audit's claim literally true for them. It is what makes re-running an ingest unable to double-apply, and what lets a genuine upstream ±1 day change be picked up rather than mistaken for a correction already made — the failure a design that shifted whatever it found stored has no way to distinguish. `tests/integration/tmdb/test_upsert_airdates.py` pins both halves.

**The correction is applied on the way in, never at read time.** `upsert_series_payload` loads the show's offsets (by `tmdb_id`, because the surrogate id they key on is minted by the show write itself) and hands them to every date it writes. A read-time correction would have to be threaded through browse, `/me/upcoming`, Watch Next, the season and episode pages and `episode_repo`'s `air_date <= today` aired filter — the many-call-sites asymmetry the specials ledger exists to police.

**`catalog/offsets.py:project_offsets` is the second writer, and it is not a re-apply step.** Ingest alone would leave a finished season wrong forever, since TMDB will never change it again. The projection re-derives `raw + offset` from the twin, so running it twice, running it after an ingest applied the same offset, and running it after the offset changed all converge — and a deleted offset row restores the upstream value, which is how retraction works at all.

**The four grains take different offsets, and that is the decision.** Episode and season take their own season's; `show.first_air_date` takes **season 1's** and `show.last_air_date` the offset of **the season holding the last dated episode**. Ted Lasso is the proof — a blanket per-show shift moves a premiere that is already right. Getting this wrong manufactures a contradiction on one page: "Premiered May 4, 2023" above a season 1 whose episode 1 says May 5.

**The oracle is TV Maze and the trust rule refuses rather than guesses.** An offset is written for a `(show, season)` only when every episode dated on both sides with an unambiguous `(season, episode)` key differs by the **same** amount, that amount is exactly **±1**, and at least **two** such episodes exist. The clamp is principled, not defensive: a timezone artifact can only ever produce ±1, and anything else is a different disagreement whose correction would make the mirror track TV Maze's schedule. Shrinking S3 is the worked example — TMDB spreads a two-episode premiere over two weeks, deltas run `+1, -6, -6`, and both the clamp and the unanimity rule reject it independently where a job trusting the oracle would have moved nine episodes by six days. That season stays a day early, on purpose, and appears in the refusal log. **A refusal leaves an existing offset alone** (it is the absence of a verdict); only a verdict of *zero* retracts. And **the comparison is always against `coalesce(tmdb_air_date, air_date)`** — reading the corrected column would make last night's correction tonight's evidence of agreement, and every offset would retract itself on the second run.

**TV Maze data is CC BY-SA 4.0, so the SPA credit returns.** NEU-1146 deleted 782k rows to shed that obligation and NEU-1147 removed the footer credit two days before this; re-admitting a TV Maze-derived value re-admits the condition. The extraction is minimised to match — **one integer per `(show, season)`**, plus since NEU-1148 **one show id per show** in `catalog.airdate_show_state`, and never TV Maze's dates, titles or numbering — and the *spine* stays free of TV Maze rows, which is what NEU-1146 was actually for. That is the line, made explicit by NEU-1145 §6.1: `catalog.show` / `season` / `episode` hold no TV Maze-derived value at any grain, while a sidecar table in the `catalog` schema holding a derived integer is a different thing from the catalog holding TV Maze rows. Do not read it as "no TV Maze value may exist inside schema `catalog`". The frontend half is its own ticket in `tvbf-frontend`.
